## Description

## Session Goal
Transform a loose set of contacts, projects, and personal opportunities into a lightweight operating system for tracking, prioritizing, and advancing high-value opportunities without letting the list become passive or aspirational.

## Key Activities
- Designed a minimal table/planilla structure for opportunity tracking with fields such as identification, type, benefit, horizon, status, next action, and priority.
- Reframed multiple contacts and projects into an operational pipeline with explicit statuses, timing, and minimum next actions.
- Distinguished between different opportunity classes: execution-ready relationships, agenda-setting institutional or academic contacts, and higher-value institutional opportunities that require a concrete value proposition before outreach.
- Added risk framing to several opportunities, emphasizing controlled pilots, documented alignment, and relationship maintenance over vague follow-up.
- Defined the work as an operational front rather than a traditional project, with inclusion/exclusion rules, progress signals, and a rhythm that prevents scope creep and endless backlog accumulation.
- Included a separate health note recommending low-intensity movement only during anorectal recovery, with avoidance of heavy lifting and high-impact strain.
- Added a mechanical diagnostic note for a front brake squeal, prioritizing inspection of pads, calipers, guides, and possible rotor contact before assuming a larger failure.

## Achievements
- Established a reusable framework for opportunity control across consulting, institutional access, [[networking]], migration, family assets, and other strategic relationships.
- Clarified that each opportunity must have a minimum next action or an explicit paused/discarded state.
- Identified that the system should measure time-worthiness and evidence of progress, not just visibility of contacts.
- Produced practical next-step framing for several concrete opportunities, reducing ambiguity around what to do next.
