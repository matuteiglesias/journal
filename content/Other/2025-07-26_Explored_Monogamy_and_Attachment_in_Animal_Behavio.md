## 📅 2025-07-26 — Session: Explored Monogamy and Attachment in Animal Behavior

**🕒 04:15–04:30**  
**🏷️ Labels**: Monogamy, Animal Behavior, Attachment Psychology, Pair Bonding, Relationship Dynamics  
**📂 Project**: Other  
**⭐ Priority**: LOW  


### Session Goal
The session aimed to reflect on monogamous behaviors in animals and explore non-jealousy and compersion in human attachment psychology.

### Key Activities
- Reviewed various animal species known for their monogamous behaviors, focusing on pair bonding, parenting practices, and social structures.
- Investigated the rarity of true genetic monogamy in nature.
- Explored the non-monogamous nature of rats, detailing their polygynandrous mating system and exceptions in lab settings.
- Discussed the concepts of non-jealousy and compersion in human attachment psychology, examining their evolutionary origins and implications for relationship dynamics.

### Achievements
- Gained insights into the diversity of mating systems across species and the complexities of pair bonding.
- Clarified the role of cultural norms in shaping human relationship dynamics.

### Pending Tasks
- Further research into the biological mechanisms underlying attachment behaviors in both animals and humans could be beneficial for deeper understanding.
