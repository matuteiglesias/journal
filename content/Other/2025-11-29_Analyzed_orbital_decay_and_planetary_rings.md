## 📅 2025-11-29 — Session: Analyzed orbital decay and planetary rings

**🕒 18:50–19:10**  
**🏷️ Labels**: Orbital Decay, Planetary Rings, Jupiter, Saturn, Earth  
**📂 Project**: Other  



### Session Goal
The session aimed to explore the orbital decay of moons around Jupiter and Mars, and to investigate the historical and current dynamics of planetary rings, particularly focusing on Earth and Saturn.

### Key Activities
- Conducted research on the orbital decay of Jupiter's moons Metis and Adrastea, comparing them with Mars' moon Phobos.
- Analyzed the inward spiral of Phobos and its implications for Mars, drawing parallels with Jupiter's inner moons.
- Investigated Earth's historical rings and their geological implications, including the Moon-forming disk and transient rings from tidal disruptions.
- Explored Saturn's rings, focusing on particle size distribution, optical depth, and visual characteristics, particularly the B ring and Cassini propeller moonlets.

### Achievements
- Gained insights into the mechanisms and timescales of orbital decay for moons around Jupiter and Mars.
- Clarified theories regarding Earth's past rings and their potential geological signatures.
- Enhanced understanding of Saturn's rings, including particle dynamics and visual appearance from different perspectives.

### Pending Tasks
- Further investigation into the uncertainties and expected outcomes of orbital decay processes.
- Additional research on the geological evidence of Earth's historical rings and their transient nature.
